{{ icon('globe',19) }} What is Soil Testing? ▾

Soil testing is the process of analyzing soil samples to measure their physical and chemical properties.

By knowing what's in your soil, you can make smarter decisions about fertilizers, irrigation, and crop selection — saving money and improving yields.

Why it matters for students

Understanding soil science is fundamental to agriculture, environmental science, and food security. Your TinyML sensor device makes real-time soil analysis accessible right in the field.

What your device measures
  • pH — Acidity or alkalinity
  • Moisture — Water content percentage
  • Temperature — Soil warmth, which drives root activity
  • Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium (N-P-K) — Core plant nutrients
{{ icon('flask',19) }} Understanding Soil pH ▾

pH is measured on a 0-14 scale: below 7 is acidic, above 7 is alkaline, and 7 is neutral. Most crops grow best between pH 6.0 and 7.0, where nutrients dissolve into a form roots can absorb.

Why extremes are a problem
  • Too acidic (below 5.5): aluminum and manganese can reach toxic levels, and phosphorus becomes hard to absorb.
  • Too alkaline (above 7.5): iron, zinc, and manganese become less available, often causing yellowing leaves.

Adding lime raises pH; adding sulfur or organic matter tends to lower it.

{{ icon('leaf',19) }} NPK Nutrients Explained ▾

N-P-K is shorthand for the three nutrients plants need in the largest amounts.

  • Nitrogen (N): fuels leafy, green growth. Too little causes yellowing; too much delays flowering.
  • Phosphorus (P): builds strong roots, flowers, and seeds.
  • Potassium (K): strengthens stems, improves drought tolerance, and boosts fruit quality.

Most field crops are comfortable with each nutrient somewhere between 40-120 mg/kg — your Sensor Readings page flags anything outside that band.

{{ icon('droplet',19) }} Moisture & Irrigation ▾

Soil moisture is reported as a percentage of water content. 50-90% is usually called "field capacity" — enough water for roots without drowning them.

Readings below 40% usually mean it's time to irrigate; readings above 95% can signal poor drainage and root-rot risk.

{{ icon('layers',19) }} Organic Matter (Estimated) ▾

Organic matter is decomposed plant and animal material — it improves water retention, feeds soil microbes, and slowly releases nutrients. Healthy fields usually sit around 3-6%.

Note: true organic-matter testing needs a lab. The number on your dashboard is a quick estimate derived from your N and K readings, clearly labeled as an estimate rather than a lab result.

{{ icon('bar-chart',19) }} Reading Your Health Score ▾

Your Health Score checks all seven readings against their optimal ranges — each one in range adds about 14 points, for a score out of 100.

  • 85-100 — Grade A: excellent, soil is in great shape
  • 65-84 — Grade B: good, minor fixes needed
  • 45-64 — Grade C: fair, needs attention
  • Below 45 — Grade D: poor, action needed soon
